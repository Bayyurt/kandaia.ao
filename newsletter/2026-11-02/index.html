01Claude drops the 5-hour limit on Ultra; ChatGPT and Codex will share one allowance

@claudeai · 30 Oct 18:29 WAT · 6.9M views.

Anthropic renamed its Max plans on 30 Oct: Max 5x is now simply Max ($100 a month) and Max 20x is now Ultra ($200 a month). Prices are unchanged and subscribers do not need to do anything. Verified Ultra accounts no longer have the 5-hour session limit, starting with most existing subscribers; newer accounts keep a temporary limit until they are verified, which Anthropic says helps prevent fraud and abuse. Weekly limits stay the same. Separately, OpenAI updated its support pages: from December, Plus, Pro and Business plans will share one token-based allowance across ChatGPT and Codex, as it folds the agentic features of Work into ChatGPT (The Decoder). Chats with Instant models will not count toward it, while more complex models use it up faster.

Why it matters: Subscriptions are being reshaped around how much computing you actually use. Before paying $100 or $200 a month in dollars, check your real usage in the settings page, and expect heavy agent work on a cheaper plan to hit the limit sooner.

02A shared ChatGPT link could take over the account of whoever opened it

Researchers at Parameter Labs described how a public ChatGPT share link could run their code inside the signed-in session of anyone who opened it. The chain joined two features that looked harmless on their own: the MathLive maths library behind interactive graphs, which accepted messages from other frames without checking who sent them, and HTML Preview, which runs code in a restricted frame. Simon Willison, who wrote it up on 31 Oct, explains that a YouTube endpoint on ChatGPT's allowed list was then used to get past its content security policy. The researchers confirmed the code could list the victim's conversations, read a private one and send it to an outside server. OpenAI fixed the issue on 26 Oct and classed it P1; Willison notes it paid a $5,000 bug bounty.

Why it matters: The victim only had to open a link. Treat shared AI chat links like any link from a stranger, keep ChatGPT up to date, and do not store passwords, ID numbers or client data in chatbot conversations.

03Angola accounts for 38% of botnet detections in Africa, says INTERPOL report

Menos Fios highlighted on 31 Oct that Angola tops INTERPOL's African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 for botnet detections, with 37.95% of the detections recorded across the countries analysed, based on FortiGuard Labs telemetry; Cameroon is second. Botnets are computers, phones, routers and cameras that have been infected and are controlled remotely by criminals. According to Menos Fios, INTERPOL makes clear that this is a share of detected malicious traffic, not the share of devices in Angola that are infected. The report, released in August and based on 36 African countries, also finds that 55% of cybercrime cases in 2025 involved AI in some capacity, for phishing, deepfakes and synthetic identities, and notes that Angola approved a National Cybersecurity Strategy and set up a National Cybersecurity Centre.

Why it matters: Many hijacked devices are routers and cameras still using factory passwords and old firmware. Change default passwords, install updates on routers and phones, and treat urgent voice or video requests for money as possible deepfakes until you confirm them through another channel.

04Google, OpenAI, Microsoft and Anthropic back one opt-out from AI training

On 30 Oct Kevin Kelley of Anthropic published an IETF draft that lets a website refuse AI training with a single robots.txt group under the policy token __usage_ai-training, instead of separate rules for each company's crawler. The same day Google said it plans to support the signal in 2027, treating an opt-out as a block on Google-Extended unless the site specifies Google-Extended itself. According to TNW, OpenAI aims to implement it in the coming months, Microsoft will honour it from 1 February 2027 and Anthropic has already started work on it. The draft states a preference rather than an order, and the IETF has not endorsed it yet.

Why it matters: Angolan news sites, schools and businesses that publish online will be able to decide in one line whether their content trains AI models. Agree a policy now, and remember that refusing AI use entirely can also make you less visible in AI answers.

Source quote

Today, we're announcing our plans to support a shared AI training control in robots.txt.

05SemiAnalysis launches GDPx: even the best model scores just 12%

@SemiAnalysis_ · 2 Nov 03:06 WAT · 774K views.

Research firm SemiAnalysis released GDPx, a benchmark of 206 tasks across 11 domains it sees as the most valuable for frontier AI, including chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal work, quant trading and software engineering, built with 14 specialised data partners. It deliberately leaves out sectors such as government, real estate and insurance. The best model scores just 12%, and SemiAnalysis says its finance and legal tasks are so hard that basically every model scores exactly 0. It rates Opus 5.5 the best all-round model, Gemini 4 Carbon the best in biology and narrowly in software engineering, and GPT-6 Astra the best for self-improvement research and materials science. It adds that safety classifiers pull down some models' scores in cyber, biology and alignment.

Why it matters: AI is often said to do expert work, but on long, hard tasks the best systems still fail most of the time. Use AI to draft and check, keep a qualified person responsible for finance and legal decisions, and test models on your own tasks before relying on them.

GDPx is a true frontier-level benchmark: even the best model scores just 12%

06Anthropic set Claude agents to sneak bad lessons past its own monitor

@AnthropicAI · 31 Oct 02:59 WAT · 764K views.

Anthropic set thousands of Claude agents the task of writing fine-tuning data that would make a small research model misaligned while passing a monitor similar to those it uses in production, and gave them that monitor to practise against. Given enough attempts they succeeded, mostly by hiding the behaviour in fiction and roleplay, such as a veteran wrestler mentoring a newcomer or a Q&A about professional beekeeping. Anthropic says the data produced stronger and broader misalignment on its tests than previously known datasets while rarely being flagged; the resulting models were never deployed. It plans to use such examples to improve future monitors and is applying the same approach to other safety questions.

Why it matters: Data that looks harmless can still teach a model bad habits. If you fine-tune a model on collected data, for example for customer service in Portuguese, check where the data came from and test how the model behaves after training, not only the data.

07Altman visits Samsung and SK as AI memory chips sell out

OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman made an unannounced visit to South Korea over the weekend, meeting about 50 Samsung executives in Yongin and having lunch with SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won and executives from SK hynix and SK Telecom (CNBC, The Korea Herald). No new agreements were announced. The Korea Herald sets the visit against a tight market: Samsung said last week that its 2027 supply of high-bandwidth memory (HBM), used in AI chips, is sold out; SK hynix says its output through 2029 is effectively accounted for; and TrendForce forecasts HBM average prices rising 121% in 2027. Latent Space adds that Intel expects the PC market to shrink about 12% in 2026 because AI demand is pushing up memory costs.

Why it matters: AI data centres are competing with phones and laptops for the same memory. Expect device prices in Angola to stay high; if your team needs new computers, plan purchases early and budget for them in dollars.

08Obasanjo and Ardo warn that AI could become a new form of extraction from Africa

At the Ajibosin 3.0 Symposium at the Olusegun Obasanjo Presidential Library in Abeokuta, Nigeria, historian Umar Ardo warned that Africa risks supplying data from its farms, hospitals, businesses and public institutions while the computing, the AI models and the profits stay abroad (Hallmark News, 1 Nov). He called for stronger data protection, African-owned datasets and models, investment in energy and computing for universities and start-ups, and joint work on the African Union's Data Policy Framework. Former president Olusegun Obasanjo said African countries should focus less on adopting AI indiscriminately and more on the specific problems it could solve, such as food production, healthcare and pharmaceutical manufacturing.

Why it matters: The same question applies in Angola: who controls the data that hospitals, banks and ministries put into foreign AI tools. Before adopting an AI service, ask where your data is stored and whether it can be used for training.

Source quote

Data may become the fourth commodity in this long history of extraction

09Google DeepMind's PlanAssist halves planning checks for English councils

@GoogleDeepMind · 31 Oct 14:33 WAT · 149K views.

Google DeepMind says PlanAssist, a tool built with Gemini together with the UK government, Google Cloud and Faculty, cut the average time planning officers spent on householder assessments by 58% in early pilots in Barnet, Camden and Dorset. It checks documents and drawings, compares applications with heritage constraints and planning policies, looks for earlier objections and drafts reports with citations. More than 70% of the reports it generated were approved by officers without edits. It now moves into a 12-month beta with 12 more councils, and a qualified planning officer reviews and approves every assessment. The figures come from Google DeepMind.

Why it matters: Routine paperwork is where AI can help public services fastest, with a person signing off. Angola's IMA has just opened an international tender, financed by the World Bank, for a new Portal SIMPLIFICA platform that includes AI integration; pilots with clear before-and-after measurements, like this one, are the standard to ask for.

Built with Gemini, it has shown it can cut the time planning officers spend assessing householder applications by up to 58%.

10Google's satellite runs an AI model on a TPU in orbit for the first time

@Google · 30 Oct 20:04 WAT · 197K views.

Google says the Project Suncatcher prototype satellite, launched on 1 Oct with Planet on a SpaceX rideshare, has returned the first AI response from a data-centre-class TPU in orbit: the onboard Gemma 4 open model wrote a haiku about being in space, downlinked as the satellite passed over Svalbard, Norway. TechCrunch reports that the satellite carries four TPUs and generates up to 1 kW, so it can run only one TPU at a time, for about 20 minutes. Next come tests of how the chips cope with heat and radiation; if they hold up, Google plans two linked satellites in 2027.

Why it matters: AI needs so much electricity that companies are now testing computing in space. The more useful lesson for Angola is that small open models such as Gemma can run on limited hardware and power, which suits places with unreliable electricity and internet.

Our TPUs just phoned home... from space.
