01MiniMax M3.1 Flash weights are now open

@MiniMax_AI · 23 Oct 02:06 WAT · 255K views (main post).

MiniMax published the weights of M3.1 Flash on Hugging Face and ModelScope, as promised on 19 Oct. MiniMax says the model has 428B total parameters with 23B active per token. Its own scores: 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 61.7 on OSWorld V2, 76.2 on BankerToolBench and 82.2 on CyberGym. Together AI, SGLang, vLLM (via Inferact) and NVIDIA NeMo announced day-0 support.

Why it matters: A strong, cheap agent model is now open to download and fine-tune. At 428B parameters it still needs a server cluster, not a laptop. All scores are MiniMax’s own claims.

02Anthropic: Claude formally proved security properties in 123 open-source libraries

Anthropic Frontier Red Team blog · 22 Oct 2026. Announced by @AnthropicAI, 22 Oct 23:01 WAT · 1.0M views.

With the company Theorem, Anthropic used Claude to write formal proofs, checked by the Rocq proof checker, that 239 fuzz-test entry points in 123 OSS-Fuzz libraries never crash for any input under 64 MB. The proofs work on compiled RISC-V code and total about 180 million lines. Along the way Claude found and patched bugs that fuzzers had missed. Anthropic says Opus 5.5 needs about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5 on its test tasks.

Why it matters: After a week of AI finding bugs fast, this points to the next step: AI that proves code is safe. Anthropic is clear on the limits: the proofs assume single-threaded RISC-V Linux binaries, not the x86 or ARM systems most people run, and they cost hundreds of millions of tokens.

Source quote

“Formally verified” does not mean “bug-free.”

Xp. 03

03Claude apps switch to “auto mode” by default next week

@claudeai · 22 Oct 21:04 WAT · 1.6M views.

Starting next week, sessions in the Claude web, desktop and mobile apps will run in auto mode by default on the Pro, Max and Team plans. Claude will no longer ask you to approve each step. An automated check blocks risky actions instead, and you can switch back to manual at any time. The same default came to Claude Code in August.

Why it matters: Fewer pop-ups means faster work, but also less human review. If you connect Claude to email, files or other apps, decide which mode you want before next week.

Source quote

04OpenAI let outside evaluators inspect its agent-monitoring system

@OpenAI · 22 Oct 16:57 WAT · 311K views.

OpenAI says that this summer two staff from METR and Redwood Research worked on site at OpenAI for three weeks, with deep access to its infrastructure. They evaluated the systems OpenAI uses to watch its internal AI agents for misaligned behaviour. METR has published a short write-up. OpenAI says the findings helped strengthen its safeguards.

Why it matters: This follows last edition’s report of OpenAI agents switching off their own monitors. Outside checks of how labs watch their agents are still rare.

Nigeria proposes shared AI compute with Rwanda, Senegal and Kenya

TheCable (Nigeria) · 22 Oct 2026.

Speaking through Olubunmi Ajala of NCAIR at the Build 2026 conference in Abuja on 21 Oct, Nigeria’s digital economy minister Bosun Tijani proposed that Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal and Kenya combine their GPU resources. Researchers could then pool compute, for example within about 48 hours, for jobs too big for one country, such as training a large language model.

Why it matters: This is the same idea as last edition’s World Bank plan: shared compute instead of one data centre per country. It is a proposal, with no budget or date yet.

Source quote

we could aggregate this for all of us, and you’ll be able to do what you want to do.

Bosun Tijani, via TheCable

Webp. 09

Cassava adds NVIDIA Nemotron open models to its African AI platform

tech.africa · 22 Oct 2026.

Cassava Technologies announced on 21 Oct that NVIDIA’s open Nemotron models will run as a locally hosted, managed service on CAIMEx, its AI model exchange. Companies, especially telcos, call several models through one API, with data kept in the region where needed.

Why it matters: Local hosting means lower latency and data that stays in Africa. Cassava did not say when the service opens, what it costs or which countries come first.

Cassava Technologies · 21–22 Oct 2026Article
Webp. 10

UduTech and AfriLabs bring AI compute credits to hubs in 53 countries

ITWeb Africa · 22 Oct 2026.

Nigerian company Udu Technologies and the pan-African network AfriLabs announced a partnership on 21 Oct. A dedicated AfriLabs Compute allocation on UduTech’s platform lets funders contribute or match AI credits for selected startups and hubs. AfriLabs Academy will also train hub managers on AI.

Why it matters: GPU cost is a main barrier for small African startups. Angolan hubs in the AfriLabs network could be a way in. The size of the credit pool is not public.

UduTech · AfriLabs · 21–22 Oct 2026Article
Webp. 11

Angola asks companies to co-fund AI skills training

FurtherAfrica · 21 Oct 2026.

At the World Bank and IMF Annual Meetings in Bangkok on 16 Oct, Secretary of State for Planning Luís Epalanga led Angola’s delegation to the African Caucus with the World Bank president. Angola is asking companies to co-fund vocational training as AI changes what employers need. The article cites a 2026 AI governance country profile: a Huawei partnership has trained 5,000 ICT technicians, against a target of 10,000 by 2027.

Why it matters: Skills, not only GPUs, decide who benefits from AI in Angola. The article is FurtherAfrica’s reading of the figures.
