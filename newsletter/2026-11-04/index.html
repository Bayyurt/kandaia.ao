01Google: schools that switch off AI will also lose Google Search for students

On 3 Nov Google said Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will be off by default for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on, with the changes starting early next year. Because Search now includes AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode, a school that wants no AI at all cannot keep Search for those students. After a backlash, Google clarified that schools can turn on Search alone without the Gemini apps, but Search keeps its AI features, now adapted to give step-by-step Guided Learning instead of quick answers. Google says 150 million students and teachers use Google Classroom worldwide (AP). Former FTC chair Lina Khan accused Google of using its search monopoly to push AI into schools.

Why it matters: Schools and universities in Angola that use Google Classroom will face the same switches. Decide now, grade by grade, what students may use AI for, and keep teaching them to check sources rather than accept the first answer on the screen.

Access to these products will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on.

02In Luanda, Africa's finance leaders say AI cannot work without power, roads and land titles

The Africa Financial Summit (AFIS) is being held in Luanda for the first time, with more than 1,200 leaders from the sector, according to RNA. On Tuesday, Samaila Zubairu, chief executive of the Africa Finance Corporation, speaking alongside the heads of the AfDB and Afreximbank, warned that AI and apps cannot formalise Africa's informal economy, which accounts for about 83% of employment, without electricity, storage, transport and working public services (TechCabal). He cited the Lobito Corridor as a project that should anchor local processing and energy, not just exports. The sixth African Financial Industry Barometer by Deloitte and Jeune Afrique, presented at the summit, found that 88% of banks and fintechs surveyed in 20 countries reported more electronic financial crime.

Why it matters: Digital tools only pay off when the basics work. For Angolan businesses this means pairing any AI project with reliable power and connectivity, and for banks and fintechs it means treating fraud prevention as part of every new digital service.

Source quote

AI is a threat, but it will not work in isolation

Samaila Zubairu, AFC · via TechCabal

03Anthropic's IPO filing: $17 billion in quarterly revenue and $895 billion in commitments

Anthropic's amended S-1, filed on 2 Nov, shows revenue of $16.96 billion in the third quarter and $33.24 billion for the first nine months of 2026, up about 1,200% on a year earlier. Quarterly growth slowed to about 47%, from 144% in the second quarter (CNBC, Reuters). The nine-month net loss was $104 billion, mostly a $94.3 billion non-cash charge on convertible notes and similar instruments; the third-quarter operating loss was $567 million. The company disclosed about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and leases, almost all for compute and infrastructure. The Wall Street Journal reported, via the New York Post, that investors are pushing for a valuation of about $1.5 to 1.6 trillion rather than $2 trillion, with the listing expected the week before Thanksgiving.

Why it matters: The companies behind the AI tools you use are spending enormous sums on computing and are under pressure to earn it back. Expect prices and plan limits to keep changing, and avoid building your work around a single provider without a backup.

substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure

04OpenAI brings in outside hackers and raises its bug bounty to as much as $172,000

@OpenAI · 3 Nov 03:29 WAT · 792K views.

OpenAI is planning its next internal security sprint with Cathedral, whose researchers will use OpenAI's models alongside its own teams to attack and test its defences. In its last sprint OpenAI dedicated 25% of its product engineers to finding and fixing weaknesses, using Codex to reproduce vulnerabilities and prepare patches. It also raised its bug bounty: a critical flaw in ChatGPT, Codex or the API can now earn up to $30,000, and exceptional findings up to $172,000 with bonuses; the new ranges apply to reports not yet paid. OpenAI says it has committed $1 billion in subsidised access over six months for defenders protecting essential services.

Why it matters: Attackers have the same AI tools as defenders. Angolan banks, telecoms and public services should test their own systems regularly and give researchers a clear, safe way to report flaws, instead of waiting for an incident.

Source quote

As AI becomes more capable at finding vulnerabilities, defenders need to put it to work before attackers do.

05Adesina proposes a $10 billion AI fund for Africa, and Enza launches an African AI fund

Former AfDB president Akinwumi Adesina proposed a $10 billion Africa AI Frontier Technology Fund on Tuesday in Abuja, designed to mobilise $50 billion over ten years for research, infrastructure and African technology companies, together with a coalition of the World Bank, development banks, governments and tech firms (BizWatch Nigeria). At the same event, the head of Nigeria's SEC said one planned data centre would need almost 80% of the country's power transmission capacity. Separately, Nairobi-based Enza Capital launched a dedicated AI Africa Fund, size not disclosed, for companies built around African languages, local data and local infrastructure; it says it has already made 11 AI investments (Empower Africa, citing TechCabal).

Why it matters: Investors are looking for African AI that solves local problems with local data, not copies of the big global models. Angolan founders who collect good data in Portuguese and national languages, and who keep running costs under control, will be better placed to raise money.

Source quote

There cannot be any AI when you are walking in the dark. Talent without infrastructure cannot compete.

Akinwumi Adesina · via BizWatch Nigeria

06AI plus HPV self-tests finds cervical precancer in 50,000 women across nine countries

A study led by ISGlobal within the PAVE consortium, published in The Lancet Obstetrics, Gynaecology & Women's Health, enrolled more than 50,000 women aged 25 to 49 between 2022 and 2025 in Brazil, Cambodia, the Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Eswatini, Honduras, Malawi, Nigeria and Tanzania. Women collected their own samples for an HPV test; those who tested positive had cervical images taken with a portable colposcope, which a deep-learning algorithm classified as severe, indeterminate or normal, with biopsies to confirm. Among nearly 49,000 valid results, more than one in four were HPV positive and over 500 severe precancers were found. The researchers say the approach allows same-day screening, triage and treatment where screening coverage is below 15%.

Why it matters: This is AI aimed at a real gap in African health care: too few specialists and patients who cannot easily return for follow-up visits. It supports health workers and still relies on biopsy to confirm, which is the model worth asking for in Angolan clinics.

Source quote

Our results demonstrate that HPV testing and automated visual evaluation provide complementary information that can guide clinical decisions

Silvia de Sanjosé, ISGlobal · via Medical Xpress

07Anthropic open-sources FlashProve, a way to prove which AI model wrote a reply

@AnthropicAI · 3 Nov 21:10 WAT · 413K views.

Anthropic released FlashProve, a research prototype that lets an AI provider commit to a model's weights and then attach a cryptographic proof (a zkSNARK) showing that a reply came from that exact model, without revealing the weights. Anyone can check the proof on an ordinary computer. For Llama 4 Maverick, a 400-billion-parameter model, a single H100 GPU produces proofs at about 110 tokens a second, around 19 MB per 1,024-token request and roughly 70 to 95 times the GPU time of running the model. The code is open under Apache 2.0 but not maintained. Anthropic says such proofs could make chats harder to hijack and let evaluators confirm the model they tested is the one served to customers.

Why it matters: Today you cannot check which model is really answering when you pay for an AI service. This is still early research, but it points to contracts where providers can prove what they deliver, something public bodies and companies buying AI should start asking about.

Source quote

FlashProve allows developers to verify whether a given response came from a given AI model.

08AI models that read and edit another model's weights directly

A paper by Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab, Sparks of Weight-Space Programming, reports that frontier models, especially GPT-6 Astra, can tell what a small neural network does just by reading its raw numbers, and can edit them. Asked to make Qwen3.5-0.8B better at maths, Astra changed 0.016% of its weights and raised its MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1%, the authors say. Simon Willison showed on 3 Nov that Qwen 3.8 27B running on his laptop can interpret the weights in one of the paper's examples. Critics such as Matthew Honnibal called the paper misleading, and Latent Space notes it is unlikely to scale beyond simple games and policies for now.

Why it matters: The surprise is that a free open model on an ordinary laptop can repeat part of the result. Students and researchers in Angola without cloud budgets can experiment with this, but treat the bigger claims with caution until others reproduce them.

Source quote

Just by looking at the raw weights, GPT-6 Astra can tell what a network was trained to do.

Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab · via Simon Willison

09Google expands staff buyouts to more countries as AI spending rises

Google confirmed to Reuters that since June it has offered voluntary exit packages to some employees in Canada, France, Germany, Japan and Switzerland, after earlier programmes in the US and UK, as it tries to contain costs amid heavy AI spending. Google Cloud and DeepMind staff were excluded from past offers. In Switzerland, employees who accepted received one month's salary per year of service, up to 12 months. Reuters adds that Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees last week.

Why it matters: Big tech companies are moving money from people to computing power. Whatever your job, the safest position is to be the person who knows how to use these tools well in your own field.

Source quote

This past summer, in June, we expanded this program globally in a few countries to support our important work ahead.

Google spokesperson · via Reuters

10DeepSeek publishes the design of a 'super-agent' that splits work across parallel workers

DeepSeek published a long design article and an open prototype repository for a 'super-agent' harness: one long-lived router session talks to people and hands out work, while isolated workers run tasks in parallel, triggered by GitHub events (Latent Space). In DeepSeek's own test, the 95th-percentile completion time fell from 29.5 to 15.7 minutes compared with a single session using subagents. DeepSeek lists the limits itself: no long-term memory across sessions yet, testing only in a small trusted internal environment, and open questions on security, privacy and agent-to-agent communication.

Why it matters: Developers in Angola can study a working design from a major lab for free. Use it to learn how to split work between agents, but do not connect it to client data or production systems while its own authors say security is unresolved.
